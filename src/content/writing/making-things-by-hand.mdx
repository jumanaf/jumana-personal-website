Most of my work lives on a screen. Data comes in, analyses go out, and the artifacts are files — real, but a little abstract. Embroidery is my antidote to that.

## The slow feedback loop

Code has a fast loop: change something, run it, see the result. Embroidery has the slowest loop I know. You commit to a stitch and you live with it. There's no undo that doesn't cost you the thread and the time.

Strangely, that constraint is relaxing. When mistakes are expensive, you slow down and pay attention — and attention turns out to be the whole point.

## Iteration, just quieter

It's still iteration. You plan, you try, you pull out a section that isn't working, you try again. The difference is that the loop runs at the speed of your hands instead of your processor, and there's something honest about that pace.

- You can't parallelize it.
- You can't optimize your way out of the tedious part.
- You just do the next stitch, and then the one after that.

## The finished thing

At the end there's an object. Not a dashboard, not a deploy — a physical thing you can hold, made entirely of small decisions.

I think that's why I keep coming back to it, and to reading, and to the other slow hobbies. They're a reminder that not everything worth doing has a fast feedback loop.
